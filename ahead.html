How to read this

A door is different from a diary entry.

Most of what follows is a meeting somebody else is holding. You can watch it, read what comes out of it, and hold it to what it said. That is worth knowing, but it is not participation.

A few entries are marked Open door. Those are consultations, calls for evidence and comment periods: a published process where an ordinary person or a small organisation can put something in writing and have it counted. Those are the ones that matter most here, because Q9 asks who the many are and how they are actually asked, and a door nobody walked through is part of that answer.

Dates are given as the source states them. Where a source says a month but not a day, the entry says so rather than inventing precision. Defence and military events are left out under the same standing rule as the watch.

Malaysia asks how its personal data law should reach artificial intelligence Open door Q9

Malaysia, online through the Unified Public Consultation platform or a published form

The Personal Data Protection Department opened Public Consultation Paper No. 1/2026 on 5 October, proposing an Artificial Intelligence and Personal Data Protection Framework, and takes comment until 23 October. The draft is a guideline under the Personal Data Protection Act 2010 rather than a new law, set out in ten parts and forty-three sections, and it follows an AI system through its whole life: planning and development, testing, deployment, monitoring and retirement. It reaches systems an organisation builds itself, buys from someone else, or uses through a cloud service or an app, and models built for general use when they are put to work on personal data. Two parts reach furthest. It says the rights a person already holds over their own data, to see it and to have it corrected, keep applying when the data has gone through an AI system. And it puts the organisation using the system on the hook for overseeing whoever supplied it, including when the data crosses a border. It sits beside the Singapore consultation, which closed the day before and is now in what already came: Singapore asked what is owed to the people whose work trained a model, and Malaysia asks what is owed to the people whose details pass through one. A separate AI governance bill is expected in Malaysia's parliament by early 2027, so this is the guideline that comes ahead of the law. Comments go through the government's Unified Public Consultation platform or a Google Form the department has published; enquiries go to risiko@pdp.gov.my. The department's own paper was not read for this scan; the detail here comes from two law firm summaries and a trade report that agree on the dates.

Pima County took 1,500 comments on its data centre rules, and the commission hears them on 28 October Q10

Pima County Planning and Zoning Commission, Tucson, Arizona, United States

The county has paused all data centre development in its unincorporated areas for four months while it writes rules, and its draft changes to Title 18 of the zoning code go to the Planning and Zoning Commission on 28 October. The Board of Supervisors is expected to vote in December, about a month before the pause lifts; no day for that vote has been published. Staff say they received between 1,200 and 1,500 comments while the code was being drafted, through an online survey, an open house on 28 September, an online forum on 8 October and email to DSDPlanning@pima.gov. What the draft asks for is specific. Any data centre larger than 25 acres would be confined to land zoned CI-2, which is about 2.3 per cent of the county, and would need a Type 3 conditional use permit, which carries two public hearings and a vote of the supervisors. Open-loop cooling, the kind that draws large volumes of water and loses it, would be banned. A developer would have to show the water exists, and preferably clean energy, though no energy source is required. Backup diesel generators would be allowed if they are newer and certified by the county's environmental quality department, with testing confined to 8am to 5pm on weekdays. The proposed noise limit is 75 decibels, about the level of a vacuum cleaner, and the proposed setback from neighbouring properties is 100 to 200 feet, about 30 to 60 metres. Two of those figures are worth setting beside Cumberland County further down this page, which proposes 45 to 60 decibels and setbacks of 500 to 1,250 feet; the two drafts are being written in the same weeks and are a long way apart. Staff asked for evidence-backed recommendations on setbacks and noise buffering in particular, and say comments that cite research are passed to the commission and the board. This carries no door chip because the reporting does not say whether the commission takes public comment on the day; the county's published agenda is where that will appear.
