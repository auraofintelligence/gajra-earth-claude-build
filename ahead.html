How to read this

A door is different from a diary entry.

Most of what follows is a meeting somebody else is holding. You can watch it, read what comes out of it, and hold it to what it said. That is worth knowing, but it is not participation.

A few entries are marked Open door. Those are consultations, calls for evidence and comment periods: a published process where an ordinary person or a small organisation can put something in writing and have it counted. Those are the ones that matter most here, because Q9 asks who the many are and how they are actually asked, and a door nobody walked through is part of that answer.

Dates are given as the source states them. Where a source says a month but not a day, the entry says so rather than inventing precision. Defence and military events are left out under the same standing rule as the watch.

Rapid City would cap a data centre by the water it draws Q10

Rapid City Planning Commission, Rapid City, South Dakota, United States

Rapid City already has data centre rules, written in December 2024, which allowed them in its light and heavy industrial districts. It is now rewriting them, and the planning commission put the revised ordinance off in September to its meeting on 5 November. The draft sorts data centres by both power and water. A hyperscale facility, at 50 megawatts or more and 10,000 gallons of water a day, would be prohibited; 10,000 gallons is about 38,000 litres, or roughly one large road tanker full. Micro data centres, up to 10 megawatts and 4,000 gallons a day, would be allowed with a 500 foot buffer from homes, and anything between the two would need a conditional use permit and a 1,000 foot buffer. Water-hungry cooling would be banned outright, the city could refuse a project on local water availability alone, the electricity company would have to sign off in writing, and on-site generators would be for emergencies only. Most drafts on this page measure a data centre by its power draw. This one sets the water line beside it and makes it just as binding.
