Entries from Ahead move here once their date passes. They are kept with what came of them, where the watch logged an outcome, because an announcement is only worth as much as what followed it.

Why this page exists

The gap between the two is the useful part.

A summit is easy to announce and easy to forget. Keeping the announcement next to the outcome makes one particular thing checkable: whether a meeting that promised inclusion delivered it, whether a consultation that opened was ever answered, whether a deadline that was set actually held.

Nothing here is deleted when it stops being current. That is the same rule the watch archive runs on, and for the same reason: a record that keeps only its wins is not a record.

California's Governor had until the last day of September to decide five data centre bills Q10

Sacramento, California, United States

The legislature had to finish by 11.59pm on 31 August, and on the final night it passed a run of bills on data centres. SB 886, from Senator Steve Padilla, would have the Public Utilities Commission set an electricity rate specifically for data centres so their costs are not passed on to other customers. SB 887 would require environmental impact reports. SB 1168, from Senator Jerry McNerney, directs the commission to examine data centre energy use against the risk of rate rises. AB 2469 would bar a local government from approving a new or expanded data centre unless the developer discloses its water plans and pays for the new water infrastructure. AB 2619 would require operators to report their water sources and use under penalty of perjury when seeking or renewing a licence or permit. The Senate's own published calendar gives 30 September as the last day for the Governor to sign or veto bills passed before 1 September, so five separate arguments were settled by that date. One thing sat behind it. The Governor vetoed a data centre water reporting bill in 2025, saying he was reluctant to impose rigid reporting requirements without understanding the full effect on business, which made AB 2619 the same question asked a second time.

What came of it. He signed, and he signed early. The bills were approved on 21 September, nine days before the deadline this entry was written against, and as a package of seven rather than the five tracked here: AB 1577, AB 2383, AB 2469 and AB 2619 alongside SB 886, SB 887 and SB 1168. AB 2619 is the water reporting requirement he refused the year before, so the second asking carried. SB 886 has the Public Utilities Commission set a data centre electricity rate by 2028 and requires the buildings to pay for the network upgrades they cause. The Governor's office calls the package the most comprehensive data centre laws in the nation, which is that office's own claim rather than a checked one; what is checkable is the count, the bill numbers and the date, and those are on the record.
