If there was more of it in the past

Precedented, never default.

Joyful responsible abundance is not a utopia waiting to be invented. The record shows it existing, repeatedly, wherever people organised for it on purpose. What it has never been, anywhere, is the default setting. That gap is the project.

The record

Where it already existed.

Record

Commons that outlived empires. Field research on common-pool resources documented communities governing shared water, forests and fisheries for centuries without either privatisation or a central state: rules made by the users, monitored by the users, with graduated consequences. Abundance held in common, responsibly, for generations.

Memory that outlived the coastline. Aboriginal oral traditions from around the Australian coast describe land that has been under the sea for more than seven thousand years, and the descriptions match the drowned geography. Knowledge carried accurately across hundreds of generations by story, song and ceremony: an information system with a longer uptime than any other on record.

Care financed by its own community. Friendly societies and mutuals paid for doctors, medicine and funerals for ordinary families before any national health system existed, owned by their members, answerable to their members. The pattern still works wherever it is allowed to.

Idle machines folding proteins. Millions of people donated the spare cycles of home computers and game consoles to medical research, for nothing, at planetary scale, and for a while that volunteer network was the most powerful computing system on Earth. Abundance made of leftovers, given for joy.

The free encyclopaedia. Written by volunteers, owned by no one, and it beat every priced alternative. The standing proof that abundance by sharing is not naive economics: sometimes it is simply the better engineering.

Custodianship that did not eat its host. Tens of thousands of years of continuous care for Country on this continent, including the island this site is strung from. The longest-running demonstration that a civilisation can be old, complex and joyful without consuming the place that holds it.

The counter-record

What more of it would have changed.

Every failure in the ledger of history reads as the trinity with a leg missing. Booms that threw a good party and left a poisoned river: abundance and joy with no name answering for the wake. Systems of duty that ground their own people to dust: responsibility with the joy removed, calling the grinding a virtue. Plenty locked behind walls until it rotted: abundance hoarded into scarcity, which is the oldest trick there is.

None of those needed different physics. They needed a third leg on the stool. That is the sense in which this project asks about the past: not nostalgia, an audit.

The past is not proof that it scales. It is proof that it exists. Scaling it on purpose, with the most capable tools ever built, is the project.
