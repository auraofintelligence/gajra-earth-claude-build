Tuesday 1 September 2026

Scanned 07:05 Brisbane (UTC+10). Seven items met the brief. Each carries the date it happened, and the searches that came back empty are listed at the end.

Japan

Version 2.0 of the Guideline for Japanese Governments' Procurements and Utilizations of Generative AI for the sake of Evolution and Innovation of Public Administration enters into force today, 1 September, having been put out for public comment from 18 March to 8 April 2026 and approved by the Council for the Promotion of a Digital Society on 12 June, and it requires every ministry to appoint a Chief Artificial Intelligence Officer, to run a risk assessment on each generative artificial intelligence system, to buy against set check sheets, and to report any project likely to be classed high risk to the Advanced AI Utilization Advisory Board.

Australia Q9

Submissions to the Senate Environment and Communications References Committee inquiry into artificial intelligence and data centres close today, 1 September, the inquiry having begun in May under the chair of Senator Sarah Hanson-Young to look at whether existing regulation keeps pace with the growth of data centres and at their effects on communities, industries and the environment, with a report due on 16 November 2026 and submissions already lodged by the Local Government Association of South Australia, the Climate Council, Climateworks Centre and the ARC Centre of Excellence for Automated Decision-Making and Society.

Australia Q9 Q10

An analysis published on 28 August argued that Aboriginal people are being left out of decisions on where artificial intelligence data centres go, citing the 120 megawatt project at Mandoon Bilya on the Helena River in Perth's eastern suburbs that was withdrawn in May 2026 before Western Australia's Development Assessment Panel could consider it, after a campaign led by the Bibbul Ngarma Aboriginal Association over Aboriginal heritage sites and the river, with more than 200 people planting over 3,000 native plants there in July, and quoting Kaley Nicholson, chief executive of the Federation of Victorian Traditional Owner Corporations, calling the current approach an investment failure, while pointing to the Karajarri People as co-owners of Western Australia's largest data centre through an Indigenous Land Use Agreement and to Traditional Owner engagement on a hyperscale project in the Northern Territory.

South Korea

The Ministry of Science and ICT announced on 28 August that it had chosen three consortiums, led by SK Telecom, KT and Kakao, from six applicants to run the AI for All project, which is to give every one of the country's roughly 52 million people free access to artificial intelligence tools, with the operators required to use models built by other companies alongside their own, receiving a combined 512 Nvidia B200 graphics processing units from the government this year and part of their running costs from 2027, agreements due to be signed in September and a full launch planned for December.

Elkhart, Indiana, United States Q9

The Elkhart city council voted nine to nil on Thursday 27 August at a special meeting to stop accepting applications for data centres and battery storage facilities until the council lifts the pause or 31 December 2027, whichever comes first, councillors saying they need the time to study the effects and to write rules on noise, water and electricity, and Mayor Rod Roberson saying it is not a rejection of data centres but a pause to look at what they mean for the community, with the pause applying to the city and not the county.

Australia

The Assistant Minister for Science, Technology and the Digital Economy, Andrew Charlton, launched the Buy Australian AI Partnership on 27 August, an eight week accelerator run by Stone and Chalk with the National AI Centre that puts Australian artificial intelligence companies in front of buyers in banking, insurance and superannuation, with the Commonwealth Bank, Westpac, ANZ, NAB and Cuscal helping participants work through enterprise procurement, assurance, regulatory requirements and deployment, the first intake aimed at financial services.

Singapore Q9

The Ministry of Law and the Intellectual Property Office of Singapore opened a public consultation on 26 August on how artificial intelligence should be handled in the country's intellectual property regime, running to 22 October and open to anyone, covering the use of copyright material to train models, copyright risk when models are deployed, what counts as human creativity in a work made with artificial intelligence, who can be named an inventor when a person and a machine work together, and what machine generated technical disclosures do to the field of prior art.

One of the seven items is from the United States and none are from Europe, so the two places this page works against over-representing account for one of seven. Three are from the Pacific, three from Asia. Four items carry an open question. The Australian item on Aboriginal exclusion carries both and is the clearest of the day for either: it names a 120 megawatt project stopped over heritage and a river, and it names two arrangements, in Western Australia and the Northern Territory, where Traditional Owners were in the room instead. The three consultation items carry Q9 because in each the door is open and the closing date is published: Australia today, Singapore on 22 October, Japan's guidelines having already been through a comment period that ran in March and April. The Elkhart pause carries Q9 because the council said in the open that it does not yet know enough to decide.

Dropped as older than the seven day window: the New Mexico Supreme Court paused the air quality permit hearing for the Oracle and OpenAI Project Jupiter campus in Dona Ana County and stopped the State Engineer allowing a well to supply the site's construction water, on a petition by the group New Energy Economy, with the hearing officer Max Shepherd recusing himself the same weekend after the Center for Biological Diversity and the New Mexico Environmental Law Center moved to disqualify him; all of that fell on 23 and 24 August, and it is the clearest thing found this week for the accounts nobody reads. Councillors in Centre Wellington, Ontario voted unanimously on 24 August for a one year data centre pause, returning for formal approval on 28 September. Kokomo, Indiana passed its data centre standards ordinance on first reading six votes to two on 24 August. A Daily Maverick investigation into South African data centre approvals ran on 24 August.

Searched and found nothing inside the window: Africa, Latin America, the Caribbean, the Middle East and North Africa, Europe and the Pacific beyond Australia, searched by name and in Spanish, Portuguese and Arabic as well as English, along with the African Union, Smart Africa, ASEAN, CARICOM, the Caribbean Telecommunications Union, the League of Arab States, the Pacific Islands Forum, the United Nations agencies and the Council of Europe. What is standing rather than moving: the Africa AI Council endorsed by Smart Africa has not issued anything since its call for nominations, Kenya's artificial intelligence bill is still before parliament and its emerging technologies policy consultation closed on 4 August, Brazil's PL 2338 sits with the Chamber of Deputies special committee and the Senate committee heard its data centre bill on 4 August, Ontario's data centre playbook consultation opened on 13 August, and the CARICOM roadmap was endorsed on 7 July. The 55th Pacific Islands Forum Leaders Meeting is sitting in Palau and carried no artificial intelligence or data centre item in this window; the Pacific Digital and Artificial Intelligence Technology Principles are still before leaders. Ahead: the Pacific Islands Forum leaders meeting runs to 4 September, Spain's data centre consultation closes on 4 September, the Eastport city council takes up its permanent ban on 9 September, the House of Lords inquiry into the United Kingdom's Online Safety Act takes written evidence until 7 September, Lubbock holds a public hearing on a citizen petition for an 18 month pause on 8 September, Kokomo's council takes its data centre ordinance up again on 14 September, Mexico's regional forums run to 17 September, Spartanburg County has its third reading on 21 September, Centre Wellington gives its pause formal approval on 28 September, Ofcom's consultation on fraudulent advertising codes closes on 2 October, the Palm Springs pause runs to 10 October, Singapore's intellectual property consultation closes on 22 October, Pataskala and Granville vote on a data centre ban on 3 November, the Australian Senate committee reports on 16 November, and the Sturgeon Lake Cree Nation's judicial review of the water licence for the Wonder Valley project in Alberta is listed for December.

Left out under the defence rule: a United Kingdom and Ukraine information sharing partnership reported on 25 August that would give the United Kingdom access to Ukrainian battlefield data through the Avengers AI Labs to train models, and a United States federal court ruling of 26 August on the Department of Defense blacklisting of Anthropic. A third was a closer call and is recorded here rather than logged: Huawei filed a bid on 26 August to build artificial intelligence data centres in Egypt, and the sectors named for it include defence, security and public surveillance alongside civil government, so it sits on the line the rule draws.
