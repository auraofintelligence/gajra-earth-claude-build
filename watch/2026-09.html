Thursday 3 September 2026

Scanned 07:06 Brisbane (UTC+10). Eight items met the brief. Each carries the date it happened, and the searches that came back empty are listed at the end.

Group of 20

United States commerce secretary Howard Lutnick said at the close of the G20 Innovation Ministerial in Chapel Hill, North Carolina on 2 September that all of the group's member nations had endorsed the Carolina Principles, a framework drafted by the United States under which governments agree to reserve new regulation for novel considerations rather than write rules specific to artificial intelligence, to direct funding towards foundational research and to widen commercial openings for emerging technologies, with White House science and technology policy director Michael Kratsios telling the meeting that policymakers do not need to approach each innovation in isolation and should not treat every emerging technology as a first of its kind policy problem, and Lutnick saying it had taken a great deal of work to get every country to agree.

European Union

The European Commission said on 1 September it had sent requests for information to more than 30 companies working in artificial intelligence, asking providers how they defend models against attack, whether independent experts have assessed them, how systems are watched after release and what the training data contains, a step that can precede a formal investigation under the AI Act, with spokesman Thomas Regnier saying the questionnaires concerned mostly safety and copyright and vice president for technology sovereignty Henna Virkkunen saying the Commission is ready to take all necessary steps to ensure companies comply with their obligations.

Chapel Hill, North Carolina, United States Q9

Several hundred students, retired staff and residents marched outside the same Chapel Hill meeting on 2 September, organised by the University of North Carolina chapters of the Sunrise Movement, Students for Justice in Palestine and the NAACP together with the Campus Y and the Party for Socialism and Liberation, calling for more government regulation of artificial intelligence and objecting to the environmental effect of the infrastructure, to surveillance cameras on campus and to the part technology executives are playing in setting policy, with speakers questioning the university's decision to host the summit.

Brazil

The Senate approved Bill 278 of 2026 on 1 September by symbolic vote, creating Redata, a special tax regime that suspends federal taxes including import duty, IPI, PIS/Pasep and Cofins on information technology equipment for companies building or expanding data centres in Brazil, with revenue forgone estimated at 7.25 billion reais to 2028, and conditions requiring all electricity to come from renewable or low emission sources, 2 per cent of the value of equipment bought to be invested in research and development inside Brazil, and at least 10 per cent of processing and storage capacity reserved for the domestic market, with a 20 per cent reduction in the investment requirement for sites in the North, Northeast and Central West; the bill now goes to the president for signature. This page logged the objection from the Coalizão Direitos na Rede and three other groups on 2 September; what has changed is that the bill has passed and the conditions attached to it are settled text.

United Kingdom Q9 Q10

The Green Party of England and Wales, led by Zack Polanski, called on 1 September for a moratorium on most new data centres across the United Kingdom with an exemption for smaller local scale sites, and asked for mandatory reporting of water and electricity use, tougher environmental standards and more community involvement in planning decisions, citing about 1,125 megalitres of water a year across roughly 200 English sites, under one per cent of non household use, and a 170 per cent rise in data centre water use in the South East between 2021 and 2025; the government rejected the moratorium, saying a halt would damage jobs and national security, and disputed the claim that data centres would be given priority over households during a shortage.

China

Two national rules touching artificial intelligence took effect on 1 September: a national standard on coordination between human and machine customer service, which makes companies legally responsible for what their automated customer service says, so that a reply being machine generated is not a defence, and requires a working transfer to a person; and management measures for micro short dramas, which require a clearly visible label on every episode made with artificial intelligence.

Egypt

Egypt's Central Auditing Organisation, whose head Mohamed Al Faisal Youssef also chairs the International Organisation of Supreme Audit Institutions, issued 15 recommendations on 1 September on how national audit bodies should govern their own use of artificial intelligence, among them choosing resource efficient models rather than assuming larger ones are better, putting data governance, cybersecurity, transparency and ethics in place before deployment, introducing the technology gradually in proportion to each body's capacity, keeping it as a support to the auditor rather than a replacement, tying each application to stated objectives with measurable indicators, and building skills at three levels: specialists, competent users and general awareness.

Shanghai Cooperation Organisation

Leaders of the ten member states signed the Bishkek Declaration on 1 September at the close of the 26th summit in Kyrgyzstan, marking the organisation's 25th year, and alongside it signed a Memorandum among the Governments of SCO Member States on Cooperation in the Field of Artificial Intelligence and a Concept for the Establishment of Regional Data Processing Centres, Computing Capacities and the Development of the Artificial Intelligence Industry within the SCO, with the declaration calling for the responsible use of artificial intelligence and the summit producing about 28 documents in all; the members signing are Belarus, China, India, Iran, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Pakistan, Russia, Tajikistan and Uzbekistan.

Nigeria

At the Lagos leg of GITEX Nigeria on 1 September the director general of the National Information Technology Development Agency, Kashifu Inuwa Abdullahi, said artificial intelligence sovereignty has to cover the whole chain from connectivity and computing capacity through data centres, cloud infrastructure and data governance to the models themselves, warning against foreign controlled systems settling into finance and health, while the Secretary to the Government of the Federation, George Akume, named three instruments the country is leaning on: Project Bridge, a fibre build of about 90,000 kilometres, the Three Million Technical Talent programme with 1.87 million registrations, and a National Digital Cloud Policy seeking 750 million United States dollars of private cloud investment over 24 months. This page logged the Abuja opening of the same event on 31 August; what is new is the sovereignty framing and the cloud policy figure.

Brazil Q9

The Coalizão Direitos na Rede, which speaks for more than 50 organisations, together with the Laboratório de Políticas Públicas e Internet, the Instituto de Defesa do Consumidor and the Rede pela Soberania Digital, published a public note on 31 August objecting to about 7.2 billion reais of tax exemptions for data centres under Bill 278 of 2026, the Redata regime, being granted before the country has rules to go with them, arguing that sovereignty means national technological and scientific capability, data security and governance, energy autonomy and protection of natural resources, and asking for more negotiation, discussions held in the open, and obligations on the companies that benefit rather than servers placed on Brazilian soil; the bill stood first on the Senate's order paper for the sitting of 1 September, and if the Senate passes it the text goes to the president for signature.

The second phase of the Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum's call for input on consumer interest and artificial intelligence closes today, 2 September, this phase asking anyone who wishes to answer about the tools, governance frameworks and regulatory approaches available to policymakers, regulators and firms for reducing harms from generative and agentic artificial intelligence while still protecting consumers, the first phase having closed on 3 July after asking people which risks they feel exposed to and what they are willing to tolerate in exchange for the benefits.

Barber County, Kansas, United States Q10

County commissioners voted two in favour, none against and one abstaining on 31 August to approve the agreement for Google's first Kansas data centre, a 1.7 million square foot site near Sharon known as Project Helium, with the agreement requiring the facility to be closed loop and air cooled rather than water cooled and to buy any other water it needs from a regulated municipal utility, the company putting the site at 500 jobs once running and initial operations from 2030, and county estimates putting property tax at close to 10 million United States dollars a year, against a state exemption available to large data centres for up to 20 years.

The city council voted unanimously on 31 August for a resolution opposing data centre development inside the city limits and calling for further study of the effects on utilities, infrastructure, public safety and the city's economic goals, with no application before it and council members pointing to the statewide pause on approvals the governor ordered on 3 August, pending an audit by the Public Utility Commission of Texas and the Electric Reliability Council of Texas, as the reason to act ahead of one.

Tuesday 1 September 2026

Scanned 07:05 Brisbane (UTC+10). Seven items met the brief. Each carries the date it happened, and the searches that came back empty are listed at the end.

Japan

Version 2.0 of the Guideline for Japanese Governments' Procurements and Utilizations of Generative AI for the sake of Evolution and Innovation of Public Administration enters into force today, 1 September, having been put out for public comment from 18 March to 8 April 2026 and approved by the Council for the Promotion of a Digital Society on 12 June, and it requires every ministry to appoint a Chief Artificial Intelligence Officer, to run a risk assessment on each generative artificial intelligence system, to buy against set check sheets, and to report any project likely to be classed high risk to the Advanced AI Utilization Advisory Board.

Australia Q9

Submissions to the Senate Environment and Communications References Committee inquiry into artificial intelligence and data centres close today, 1 September, the inquiry having begun in May under the chair of Senator Sarah Hanson-Young to look at whether existing regulation keeps pace with the growth of data centres and at their effects on communities, industries and the environment, with a report due on 16 November 2026 and submissions already lodged by the Local Government Association of South Australia, the Climate Council, Climateworks Centre and the ARC Centre of Excellence for Automated Decision-Making and Society.

Australia Q9 Q10

An analysis published on 28 August argued that Aboriginal people are being left out of decisions on where artificial intelligence data centres go, citing the 120 megawatt project at Mandoon Bilya on the Helena River in Perth's eastern suburbs that was withdrawn in May 2026 before Western Australia's Development Assessment Panel could consider it, after a campaign led by the Bibbul Ngarma Aboriginal Association over Aboriginal heritage sites and the river, with more than 200 people planting over 3,000 native plants there in July, and quoting Kaley Nicholson, chief executive of the Federation of Victorian Traditional Owner Corporations, calling the current approach an investment failure, while pointing to the Karajarri People as co-owners of Western Australia's largest data centre through an Indigenous Land Use Agreement and to Traditional Owner engagement on a hyperscale project in the Northern Territory.

South Korea

The Ministry of Science and ICT announced on 28 August that it had chosen three consortiums, led by SK Telecom, KT and Kakao, from six applicants to run the AI for All project, which is to give every one of the country's roughly 52 million people free access to artificial intelligence tools, with the operators required to use models built by other companies alongside their own, receiving a combined 512 Nvidia B200 graphics processing units from the government this year and part of their running costs from 2027, agreements due to be signed in September and a full launch planned for December.

Elkhart, Indiana, United States Q9

The Elkhart city council voted nine to nil on Thursday 27 August at a special meeting to stop accepting applications for data centres and battery storage facilities until the council lifts the pause or 31 December 2027, whichever comes first, councillors saying they need the time to study the effects and to write rules on noise, water and electricity, and Mayor Rod Roberson saying it is not a rejection of data centres but a pause to look at what they mean for the community, with the pause applying to the city and not the county.

Australia

The Assistant Minister for Science, Technology and the Digital Economy, Andrew Charlton, launched the Buy Australian AI Partnership on 27 August, an eight week accelerator run by Stone and Chalk with the National AI Centre that puts Australian artificial intelligence companies in front of buyers in banking, insurance and superannuation, with the Commonwealth Bank, Westpac, ANZ, NAB and Cuscal helping participants work through enterprise procurement, assurance, regulatory requirements and deployment, the first intake aimed at financial services.

Singapore Q9

The Ministry of Law and the Intellectual Property Office of Singapore opened a public consultation on 26 August on how artificial intelligence should be handled in the country's intellectual property regime, running to 22 October and open to anyone, covering the use of copyright material to train models, copyright risk when models are deployed, what counts as human creativity in a work made with artificial intelligence, who can be named an inventor when a person and a machine work together, and what machine generated technical disclosures do to the field of prior art.
